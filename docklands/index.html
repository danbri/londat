How to use it

One finger: turn and tilt. Two fingers: move, zoom, twist. Tap a building or a marker: its record. Press and hold: route from or to that point. Drag the time wheels at the top: the upper one sets the time of day, the lower one the day of the year; "Now" returns to the time now.

Ground, building heights and open track are measured (EA LiDAR 1 m). The depth gauge (Layers > Below ground > Depth gauge, or Views > Underground) is at the right edge: drag it down to remove everything higher than that level, so you can look into the ground. Basement depths and indoor levels use OSM and Wikidata storey counts × the storey height setting. Tunnel levels are modelled between known levels: portals and open cuts the LiDAR can see, and published station levels (with sources).

Pixel art (Look > Style): drag to move, pinch or wheel to zoom, twist two fingers to turn. The arrows at the top right turn the view by a quarter.

Your location

The round button at the bottom right shows where you are, as in a phone's map app (also Go > My location). Tap it once to centre the view on you (the browser asks first), again to turn the view with your phone's compass, again to stop turning; drag the map and it stops following. The eye button that then shows puts the view at your eye height, and you look round by turning the phone. Hold the button to turn location off. Your position and heading stay on this device: the page sends them nowhere, stores them nowhere and does not put them in the address.

Live data

Nothing is fetched until you press the button. By default the tide, weather, lines and storm overflows come from the hourly londat cache (one file, no request to the publishers; each line gives its fetch time) and traffic camera positions from the last snapshot; air quality always comes from the London Air Quality Network. Tick "Live" to ask each publisher's open API directly (no key); a theme older than an hour in the cache is also asked directly. The full catalogue of checked sources is at feeds. The river in the model follows the tide prediction (Time > Tide).
