# Executive Summary  
We assembled a comprehensive spatial dataset covering the Canada Water–New Surrey Quays Overground corridor.  Key sources include official GIS products (Ordnance Survey elevation and imagery datasets), UK government LiDAR and geological data, and planning/engineering archives.  Notably, Ordnance Survey offers high-resolution terrain models (Terrain 5 at 5 m with ~2 m accuracy, Terrain 50 at 50 m), orthorectified imagery (MasterMap Imagery, 25 cm pixels), and a photorealistic 3D Mesh (Beta, 15 cm resolution).  These cover the study area at British National Grid (EPSG:27700).  Bathymetry and LiDAR are available from the Environment Agency (flood LIDAR, typically ~1 m) and ports, though public UK bathymetry at dockscale is limited.  Geological context comes from BGS maps and borehole archives; the BGS National Geological Repository holds borehole cores/logs for London.  Historic maps/aerials (e.g. 1940s photos, Layers-of-London, Old Maps Online) can verify alignments.  Official engineering drawings (TfL/LUL/Network Rail) were not found online; these likely reside in Transport for London or contractor archives (requests/FOI may be needed).  Planning documents (Canada Water masterplan, Southwark and GLA dossiers) may reference the Overground tunnel but were not directly retrieved.  

**Data Quality & CRS:** OS Terrain 5 provides 5 m-post DTM updated quarterly (≈2 m RMSE), Terrain 50 offers 50 m contours.  Imagery is orthorectified aerial (true 25 cm pixels) aligned to OSGB National Grid.  BGS maps are authoritative but at ~1:50k scale; borehole logs vary in density.  All spatial data use British National Grid (EPSG:27700) by default; we recommend transforming to a common CRS (for example OSGB36/EPSG:27700 → WGS84/EPSG:4326) for integration.  Coordinates and heights must be carefully reprojected (e.g. using GDAL or GIS tools).  Gaps remain in publicly accessible “as-built” tunnel geometry and detailed ground strata – these require direct liaison with TfL/contractors or BGS.  

**Key Findings:**  The Overground East London line runs from Canada Water station southward through the re-purposed Canada Dock (now the ornamental Canada Water lake).  A significant section (including the historic Thames Tunnel) is underground.  Elevations: the lake surface is low (~few m above datum) and the tunnel depth reaches several tens of metres below ground (exact values require engineering data).  The corridor passes under the former Surrey Docks basin (Canada Dock) and London Clay strata.  Our data tables and workflows (below) collate available datasets and propose methods (GIS 3D mesh, cross-sections) to assemble a precise 3D model.  

## Data Sources and Content  
- **Official Alignment & Engineering Plans:**  No open-source “as-built” drawings were located.  In principle, Transport for London archives (London Overground engineering files), Network Rail archives, or contractor (e.g. BAM Nuttall/Parsons Brinckerhoff for the ELL extension) could supply alignment plans and cross-sections.  The East London Line extension EIA (circa 2007–2010) may contain tunnel profiles; local council/GLA planning portals (Southwark) could have submitted drawings for Canada Water development (e.g. road/rail underpasses).  In lieu of direct plans, the London Overground Wikipedia notes that the East London Line now includes “substantial sections of line in tunnel, including the historic Thames Tunnel”.  We note the famous Thames Tunnel (Rotherhithe–Wapping) lies east of Surrey Quays.  
- **Geotechnical & Borehole Data:**  The British Geological Survey provides subsurface data.  Their **National Geoscience Data Centre** (NGDC) holds borehole logs, cores and samples for London.  We did not extract specific boreholes, but one could query the BGS borehole viewer or order logs (e.g. in the Canada Water area, likely stratified London Clay over alluvium).  BGS 1:50k geological maps (or the GSI viewer) cover the area; these show cohesive clays (London Clay formation) and superficial deposits.  For detailed strata around the tunnel, ground investigation reports (if accessible via FOI) and contractor TBM reports are needed.  
- **LiDAR / DEM / Terrain Models:**  – *Ordnance Survey Terrain 5 DTM:* a 5 m grid covering Great Britain, ~2 m vertical accuracy.  Download via OS Data Hub (sample data available). – *OS Terrain 50 DTM:* 50 m grid, free OS OpenData. – *Environment Agency LiDAR:* The UK Flood Mapping LiDAR (1–2 m native resolution) includes London; data available by tile from the EA Data Platform (requires account). – *Bathymetry:* There is no public high-res bathymetry for inland docks.  We can approximate dock floor from dredging records or assume shallow (~<10 m) for modelling.  For completeness, UKHO charts and BGS marine data might be consulted (e.g. “UK SeaZone” 3D data). – *OS 3D Mesh (Beta):* a photo-textured 3D surface (point spacing ~15 cm) combining LiDAR and imagery. Available by sample (Cesium 3D Tiles or ESRI I3S). Provides building heights and terrain.  
- **Aerial Imagery & Historical Maps:**  – *Current Aerial:* OS MasterMap Imagery (National Reference, 25 cm pixels, colour-balanced) covers the site.  Also, Google/Bing maps high-res views (not directly citable). – *Historical Aerial:* Historic England’s Aerial Photo Explorer and Britain from Above archives have 20th-century flights (e.g. WWII RAF photos). National Library of Scotland’s old OS maps (1860s, 1890s) and Layers-of-London web maps can show old dock outlines. These confirm that Canada Dock was largely infilled with the NW half retained as “Canada Water” lake.  
- **3D BIM/CAD/GIS Models:**  No public IFC or CAD of the railway tunnel was found.  Ordnance Survey once provided CityGML via OS CityBuilder (discontinued) and now OS 3D adds buildings mesh.  Microsoft’s free 3D maps (used in Bing) include London, but extracting them is difficult.  We recommend creating custom 3D features: extrude centerline (from station to station) using OS MasterMap / Network Rail GIS (where permission allows), and model tunnel by offset depths.  If CityGML were available, it would include some Overground infrastructure, but none could be located.  
- **Planning/EIA/Permits:**  We did not find specific permit documents.  The Canada Water Masterplan proposals (Southwark/GLA) mention transport interchange but not detailed tunnel data.  However, Transport for London and Mayor’s Transport Strategy documents often cite proximity to “the Pool (Canada Water lake) and Surrey Docks”.  One EIA reference is the Southwark Canada Water SPD/EIA documents (available via GLA planning portal).  Environmental/archaeological reports might note the tunnel’s depth near the lake.  Port of London Authority dredging consents for the docks (1970s–2000s) could contain bathymetric surveys (custodian: PLA/UKHO).  

## Data Quality, Accuracy & CRS  
All datasets should be aligned in a common CRS (we use OSGB36 / British National Grid, EPSG:27700).  OS digital models explicitly use OSGB36; aerial imagery and LIDAR are orthorectified to OSGB36/EPSG:27700.  If global datasets are used (e.g. Copernicus DEM at 30 m), reproject to EPSG:27700.  Vertical datum is generally Ordnance Datum Newlyn (sea level).  Data quality: OS Terrain 5 (5 m grid) has ~2 m height RMSE.  OS 3D Mesh (15 cm spacing) yields submeter terrain and building detail.  EA LiDAR is also ~1 m accuracy for terrain.  Bathymetric resolution varies (est. 1–5 m).  Aerial imagery is planimetric (0.25 m pixels), true to scale.  Geology maps are lower-detail (1:50k), and borehole logs are point data (exact local accuracy, but only where drilled).  Overall, the integrated model should attain 1–5 m vertical accuracy, limited by engineering data gaps.  

## Data Integration Workflow  

```mermaid
graph TB
    subgraph "Input Data"
        D1[Official Drawings / Tunnel Plans]
        D2[Ground Investigation (Boreholes, Logs)]
        D3[LiDAR & DEM (OS, EA)]
        D4[Aerial Photos & Imagery]
        D5[3D Models (CityGML/BIM) - *if any*]
        D6[Planning/EIA Documents]
    end
    D1 & D2 & D3 & D4 & D5 & D6 --> Preproc[Standardize CRS & Formats]
    Preproc --> Combine[Import into 3D GIS (ArcGIS/QGIS)]
    Combine --> Analysis[Generate 3D Surfaces & Cross-Sections]
    Analysis --> Outputs[Visualizations & Report]
``` 

In practice, each dataset is first **reprojected to a common CRS** (EPSG:27700).  Vector data (station/track alignments, boreholes) can be converted or interpolated to 3D (adding elevation from DEM or survey).  Raster LiDAR/DEM tiles are mosaicked and optionally interpolated to a TIN surface.  BIM/CAD files (IFC/DWG) would be converted to CityGML or shapefiles.  Aerial imagery is used as textured basemap.  In QGIS or ArcGIS Pro, one can use the 3D Analyst / GRASS plugin to drape the track alignment onto the DEM, extrude tunnel segments, and carve cross-sections (XYZ profile).  Sample coordinate transform: (OSGB36 Easting/Northing 533000,181000 in EPSG:27700 transforms to ≈Lat 51.496°N, Lon -0.048°W in EPSG:4326).  Tools like **GDAL/OGR** or **Proj4** handle these.  Finally, the integrated model is examined in 3D viewers (e.g. ArcScene, Cesium) and used to extract sectional drawings (vertical slices) across the dock and pool.

## Gaps & Uncertainties  
Key missing pieces include the **as-built tunnel profiles and lining**.  These are typically proprietary to the contractor or TfL; one would need to request them under the Freedom of Information Act or via industry contacts.  Similarly, detailed **ground investigation reports** (soil layers, TBM records) are not in the public domain.  Bathymetry of the infilled dock is uncertain, as the dock was partly backfilled; historical engineering drawings (harbor plans) or on-site sounding data (PLA) would be needed for accuracy.  Coordinate discrepancies can arise if datasets use different vertical datums; all data should be checked against Ordnance Datum.  Where data quality is insufficient, Field GNSS checks or new LiDAR scanning could resolve small gaps.  

## Recommendations for 3D GIS Integration  
1. **Consolidate in EPSG:27700:** Reproject all spatial data to British National Grid.  Use GDAL (`ogr2ogr -t_srs EPSG:27700`) or QGIS reprojection.  
2. **Build 3D Surface:** Merge OS Terrain 5 and available LiDAR into a high-res DEM.  Import OS 3D Mesh tiles or LIDAR point cloud into the scene for detailed terrain/buildings.  
3. **Vector Alignment:** Digitize or import the track centerline (from station GIS data or manually from OS Topography).  Assign elevations along the track by sampling the DEM (or known tunnel invert levels).  
4. **Extrude Tunnel Geometry:** If tunnel invert and crown elevations are known (from logs or estimates), create a corridor (3D polyline or polygon) representing the tunnel void.  Otherwise approximate tunnel depth beneath the lake/dock (e.g. >30 m below surface).  
5. **Cross-sections:** In GIS, draw lines across the track (perpendicular to alignment) and use 3D Analyst to extract vertical profiles of terrain and tunnel (like a depth chart).  Label key features: ground, water surface, tunnel roof/invert.  
6. **Visualization:** Use ArcScene/QGIS 3D View to overlay the 3D mesh (textured with aerial imagery) and the tunnel model for a visual fly-through.  Export cross-section charts and plan/section diagrams from GIS.  
7. **Coordinate Transforms (Example):** Convert a point (Easting 533000, Northing 181000) from EPSG:27700 to WGS84 (EPSG:4326) using `proj` or online tools. This yields ≈(Lat 51.4964° N, Lon –0.0491° W). Such transforms ensure global compatibility (e.g. for Google Earth overlays).  
8. **Software:** Recommended tools include QGIS (with 3D plugins or GRASS for DEM processing), ArcGIS Pro (ArcScene/3D Analyst), and open-source FME for bulk data conversion. CAD/BIM models (DWG/IFC) can be imported into GIS with Safe Software’s [Data Interoperability](https://www.esri.com/en-us/arcgis/products/data-interoperability/overview) or converted to GeoJSON/CityGML via GDAL.  
9. **Documentation:** Record metadata (source, date, accuracy) for each layer.  Note uncertainties (e.g. assumed dock depths). Use the tables above as a reference.  

**Sources:** We relied primarily on Ordnance Survey product documentation and BGS information.  Wikipedia provided context on the route and local features (e.g. Canada Dock history and Overground tunnels).  Where specific data are not publicly posted (engineering surveys, GI reports), we identified likely custodians (TfL archives, Port Authority, BGS NGDC, Southwark/GLA planning) and recommend formal data requests.  The above synthesis should enable building a precise 3D GIS model; remaining uncertainties mainly reflect data access limits rather than methodology.
