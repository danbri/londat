Canary Wharf and the Isle of Dogs, from open data

Everything collected for this project, joined in one place: 1,129 buildings with internal ids and their occupants, owners, homes and registered companies; every possible E14 postcode; published levels below ground; museum and heritage records; the Thames bed and its flood walls; and 324 live feeds and event sources. Tap a number to explore it. The 3D models are separate pages: Docklands 3D and Canada Water corridor. The knowledge graph search page holds the same records as linked data.

Now

The page asks no outside service for data until you press the button. Tide, weather, lines and storm overflows then come from the hourly londat cache (air quality from the London Air Quality Network); tick "Live" to ask each publisher directly.

Layers

Tap a feature for its record. Basemap and building outlines: © OpenStreetMap contributors (ODbL). Other layers: see Data and licences.

Your location: the round button at the bottom right of the map asks for it only when you tap it, then centres the map on a blue dot (a drag stops following; hold the button to turn it off). The page sends your position nowhere, stores it nowhere and does not put it in the address. As with any place you look at, the map tiles around it come from tile.openstreetmap.org.

Buildings

Every OSM building outline in the Canary Wharf box has an internal id (cwb-NNNN) that stays the same between rebuilds. Heights come from OSM, the EA LiDAR or Wikidata; floors from OSM building:levels or Wikidata.

Businesses

Occupants come from OSM, the FSA food hygiene file, Wikidata and the Canary Wharf Group directory (Internet Archive copies). Chain-store branches are matched to the OSM name-suggestion-index. Registered companies come from Companies House: a registered office is not proof that a company works there.

Postcodes

Every syntactically valid postcode in E14 (4,000 candidates), checked against the ONS Postcode Directory (August 2026). "Never allocated" codes are the pool that new buildings will get. Introductions and terminations are an official record of change. Method: postcode page.

Below ground

Published levels, in metres above Ordnance Datum Newlyn (m OD), on one scale. The Elizabeth line box at Canary Wharf was built in the drained West India Dock: its base slab is 25 m below the dock water. Every value links to its source. Tower roofs are drawn at a compressed scale.

Basements

Floors below ground from OSM building:levels:underground or Wikidata. The 3D model takes 4 m per floor; that is an assumption.

    London Datastore, clipped to the zone

    Every dataset of the London Datastore (data.london.gov.uk) was triaged by written rules; the open ones that hold data for the Docklands model box were harvested here, zone rows and features only. Tap "map" for a layer on the map; each layer's records name their source, dates and licence. Building facts (heat demand, solar potential, Census context of the LSOA, venues) are in each building's record. Method: README.

    Data quality

    The same checks run on every rebuild, in eight classes. They measure where sources disagree, where joins fail and where our own tools damage the data, so that the rules for combining sources are built on measured error classes. Nothing here is corrected automatically. Analysis and proposed compositing rules: README · all tables: CATALOGUE.
