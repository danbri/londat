    Gaussian splats

    Splats are drawn back to front and tested against the model's depth, so towers, glow outlines and routes stay in front of or behind them correctly. "Splats only" keeps the model in the depth buffer without drawing it.

    Music: the towers as a visualiser

    Each building follows one of 24 frequency bands, west (bass) to east (treble). In pixel art the buildings themselves stretch, twist and sway; in the map style the splat towers do ("Splats only" starts on the estate).

    Glow: who is inside

    Buildings glow when a source states that such an occupant is inside: OSM tags, Wikidata classes, the FSA business type or the Canary Wharf Group directory. Registered offices are not counted. Wikidata occupant links have no dates, so some are former tenants.

    Ground, building heights and open track are measured (EA LiDAR 1 m). The depth gauge ("Below ground" in the menu) removes everything higher than the level you set, so you can look into the ground; the ground and water stay as faint context. Basement depths and indoor levels use OSM and Wikidata storey counts × the storey height setting. Tunnel levels are modelled between known levels: portals and open cuts the LiDAR can see, and published station levels (yellow labels, with sources).

    How to use it

    One finger: rotate. Two fingers: move, zoom, and twist to turn. Tap a building or label: its record. Press and hold: route from or to that point. The menu (top left) has the views, the layers, the route finder and this page. "Below ground" in the menu shows the depth gauge on the left: drag it down to cut away everything above that level.

    Isometric pixel art (Layers, Style): drag to move, pinch or wheel to zoom, twist two fingers to turn, move two fingers up or down to tilt. With a mouse: right or Shift drag turns and tilts. The arrows at the top right turn the view by a quarter.

    Your location

    The round button at the bottom right shows where you are, as in a phone's map app. Tap it once to centre the view on you (the browser asks first), again to turn the view with your phone's compass, again to stop turning; drag the map and it stops following. The eye button that then shows puts the view at your eye height, and you look round by turning the phone. Hold the button to turn location off. Your position and heading stay on this device: the page sends them nowhere, stores them nowhere and does not put them in the address.

    Live data

    Nothing is fetched until you press the button. By default the tide, weather, lines and storm overflows come from the hourly londat cache (one file, no request to the publishers; each line gives its fetch time) and traffic camera positions from the last snapshot; air quality always comes from the London Air Quality Network. Tick "Live" to ask each publisher's open API directly (no key); a theme older than an hour in the cache is also asked directly. The full catalogue of checked sources is at feeds.
